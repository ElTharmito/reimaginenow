Undi18

We lowered the voting age by amending the Federal Constitution.

Malaysia’s voting age was set in the Federal Constitution, so changing it needed a two-thirds majority in both houses of Parliament. Our founders started Undi18 from the outside, with no budget, no party behind them and no institutional access, and spent three years building support across benches that agreed on very little else, until voting against it became the harder position.

The bill passed 211 to nil. It is the first bill in Malaysia’s history to clear both houses of Parliament unanimously, and it put 7.8 million more Malaysians on the electoral roll.

We started Reimagine Now to do the same thing for clients.

What we do

We do our own research, write our own strategy, and run our own campaigns.

When the people running the campaign are the same people who did the polling, you find out within days that a message is not landing, and you change it that week instead of at the next quarterly review.

Sentiment analysis

We establish where opinion sits, who holds it and how hard they hold it, before anyone writes a word.

  • Social listeningWe track Facebook, X, Instagram, YouTube, TikTok, forums, blogs and online news for your issue, with an alert threshold set so you hear about a problem while it is still small enough to handle.
  • PollingOpinion surveys broken down by age, income, locality and region. This is usually where a client finds out that the issue they thought was national is confined to two states, or that the group opposing them is smaller than the noise suggests.
  • Issue impact analysisOur own scoring framework. It ranks every live issue by how far it has spread and how hard people are engaging with it, so you can tell which ones need answering and which will burn out on their own.

Strategy

We work out what to say, who needs to hear it, and who has to be brought along for any of it to hold.

  • Communications strategyWe settle four things in a fixed order: the image you are trying to hold, the narrative that carries it, the issues you will engage on, and the channels you will use. Campaigns drift when they are built in the wrong order.
  • Audience segmentationWho you are actually talking to, where they are, and which arguments move them. In practice this is almost always a smaller group than the client assumed.
  • Stakeholder managementWe engage the government and political stakeholders around your issue, both to find out what is actually happening inside and to move your position with the people who decide it.

Delivery

We run the campaign ourselves and stay on the account for as long as it runs.

  • Social media targetingPaid and organic, segmented down to constituency level where the issue calls for it.

Track record

What the work actually looks like.

Our client work is confidential, so these are written without names. What we can show you is how we read each problem and what we did about it.

Healthcare

The problem
A global pharmaceutical company needed a health ministry to put real money behind a disease that was serious, widespread and nowhere near the top of the political agenda.
The read
The ask was reasonable, but there was no version of the client making it directly that did not look like a company lobbying for its own market. Whatever the merits, that is the story that gets written.
The move
So we did not make the ask. We built a national youth programme around the disease and ran it as an open innovation challenge, which handed the ministry a platform it wanted to be seen on and a youth constituency it wanted to reach, and kept the client out of the framing.
The result
The ministry’s two most senior officials came in voluntarily, the disease moved up the agenda as a public health priority rather than an industry request, and the programme itself produced 50 trained ambassadors and 10 workable solutions.

Energy

The problem
A national energy liberalisation programme had to be explained to a public that did not want it, did not trust the pricing behind it and had no interest in the economics.
The read
Arguing the policy on its merits was a losing position, because the public was not arguing about the policy. They were reacting to a price they had no say in setting, and explanation does not answer a grievance about not being consulted.
The move
We built a standing platform for young people to take part in energy and electricity policy, and brought the delivering agencies into it directly, so the programme had somewhere to be discussed rather than only announced.
The result
The argument moved from price to participation, a programme with no public constituency ended up with one, and the platform’s own programmes ran 120 per cent above their engagement target.
